# Prescription Creation and Record Entry Process

One of the most important functions of the platform is storing **prescriptions and treatment records** generated during hospital visits.

In real-world hospitals, doctors typically write prescriptions on paper after examining the patient. The doctor does not usually enter data directly into a computer system. Instead, the responsibility of digitizing this information often falls to a **medical assistant or nurse**.

The platform follows a similar workflow to match real-world healthcare practices.

---

# Real-Life Workflow Modeled in the System

The process of adding prescriptions to the system involves several participants.

The main actors involved are:

Patient
Doctor
Medical Assistant / Nurse

Each role contributes to a different part of the process.

---

# Step 1: Patient Visits Doctor

The process begins when a patient visits a doctor at a hospital.

Before the doctor can access the patient's digital medical history, the patient must grant access using one of the available authentication methods.

The patient may grant access using either:

OTP authentication
QR code confirmation

Once access is granted, the doctor can view the patient's existing medical records.

---

# Step 2: Doctor Examination

The doctor examines the patient and determines the diagnosis and treatment plan.

After completing the examination, the doctor writes the prescription and medical instructions.

In many real-world hospitals, this prescription is written on paper.

The prescription typically contains:

Diagnosis details
Medication instructions
Recommended tests
Lifestyle advice
Follow-up visit date

The doctor may also instruct the nurse regarding additional information that needs to be recorded.

---

# Step 3: Nurse Digitizes the Prescription

After the consultation, the patient provides the prescription to the nurse or medical assistant.

The nurse then creates a **digital record of the visit** inside the system.

The nurse is responsible for converting the doctor's handwritten prescription into structured digital data.

This process ensures that medical records become searchable and organized.

---

# Step 4: Creating a Visit Record

When the nurse creates a new record in the system, the following information should be entered:

Patient identifier
Hospital identifier
Doctor identifier
Nurse identifier
Date of visit

These identifiers ensure that the visit record is correctly linked to the patient and healthcare providers.

---

# Step 5: Entering Medical Details

The nurse then enters important information from the prescription into structured fields.

Examples of information that may be recorded include:

Diagnosis
Symptoms observed
Medication names
Dosage instructions
Recommended medical tests
Next visit date

Structured fields allow the system to analyze and visualize health trends in the future.

For example, certain conditions such as diabetes may include numerical values such as blood sugar levels.

These values can later be used to generate health graphs.

---

# Step 6: Uploading the Prescription Document

In addition to structured data, the nurse should upload a digital copy of the original prescription.

The prescription may be uploaded as a scanned document or photograph converted into a PDF file.

This document serves as an official copy of the doctor's prescription.

The uploaded file becomes part of the patient's permanent medical record.

---

# Step 7: Saving the Record

Once the nurse finishes entering the required information and uploading the prescription document, the system stores the record in the patient's profile.

The record is organized according to the following hierarchy.

Patient → Hospital → Doctor → Visit Record

This structure helps patients easily navigate their treatment history.

---

# Step 8: Patient Access to the Record

After the record is created, the patient can immediately view the new prescription from their dashboard.

The patient should be able to see:

Hospital name
Doctor name
Date of visit
Diagnosis summary
Prescription document

Patients can review these records whenever necessary.

---

# Step 9: Record Transparency

To maintain trust and accountability, the system records metadata for each prescription entry.

Each record should display:

Doctor who treated the patient
Nurse who created the digital entry
Time of record creation

If the record is modified later, the system should also log the modification details.

These logs help patients understand how and when their medical data was updated.

---

# Step 10: Editing or Updating Prescriptions

Medical records should not be casually modified once they are created.

However, there may be situations where corrections are necessary.

Possible scenarios include:

Incorrect data entry
Missing information
Additional test results

If edits are allowed, the system should maintain a **clear history of changes**.

Each modification must be recorded in the activity log.

Patients and doctors should be able to view these logs.

---

# Step 11: Relationship Between Prescriptions and Future Visits

Many prescriptions include instructions for follow-up visits.

When the nurse enters the next visit date, the system stores this information.

The platform may later notify the patient about the upcoming appointment.

This feature helps patients remember important medical follow-ups.

---

# Why This Process is Important

The prescription entry process is the **main mechanism through which medical records enter the platform**.

By combining structured medical data with uploaded documents, the system ensures that records remain both:

medically accurate
digitally organized

This process also ensures that healthcare providers maintain control over medical documentation while allowing patients to access their records easily.
