# Doctor Role – Complete Functional Description

---

# 1. Overview of the Doctor Role

The doctor is one of the primary medical professionals in the system and plays a central role in patient care.

Doctors use the platform to access patient medical histories, review past prescriptions, and manage their list of treated patients.

Doctors do not directly manage hospital administration or create institutional structures. Instead, they operate as **independent professionals who can associate themselves with hospitals and interact with patients during medical consultations**.

In this system, doctors maintain their **own professional identity**, which allows them to continue using the same account even if they change hospitals.

Doctors are responsible for:

Viewing patient records
Reviewing treatment histories
Working with nurses to create digital medical records
Tracking patients they have treated

Doctors **cannot modify or delete existing medical records** to maintain the integrity of healthcare data.

---

# 2. Doctor Profile Creation

Doctors create their own professional accounts on the platform.

This ensures that each doctor maintains a **single identity across the system**.

During account creation, doctors provide professional information such as:

Full name
Email address
Password
Medical specialization

Examples of medical specialization include:

Dermatology
Orthopedics
Cardiology
General Medicine

After creating the account, the doctor can log in to the platform.

---

# 3. Doctor Login

Doctors log in using their email and password.

After successful authentication, the doctor is redirected to the **Doctor Dashboard**.

The doctor dashboard acts as the main workspace where doctors manage their patients and view medical records.

---

# 4. Doctor Dashboard Overview

The doctor dashboard should provide quick access to important information.

Typical sections of the doctor dashboard include:

Doctor profile
Associated hospitals
Patient list
Patient access requests
Medical records
Audit logs

The dashboard should provide an organized view of all patients treated by the doctor.

---

# 5. Joining a Hospital

Doctors operate independently but may associate themselves with hospitals where they work.

To join a hospital, doctors must use the **hospital code provided by the institution**.

The joining process works as follows:

The doctor logs into the platform.

The doctor enters the hospital code.

The system sends a join request to the hospital.

The hospital administrator reviews the request.

If approved, the doctor becomes associated with that hospital.

This association allows the doctor to work within that hospital’s workflow and collaborate with nurses assigned to them.

Doctors may later be removed from a hospital if their employment ends.

---

# 6. Accessing Patient Records

Doctors cannot access patient medical records without permission.

The system uses **patient-controlled access** to protect patient privacy.

When a patient visits a doctor, the patient must grant access to their records.

This ensures that only authorized doctors can view sensitive medical information.

---

# 7. Patient Access Methods

There are two primary methods through which doctors gain access to patient records.

---

## OTP Access

The doctor requests the patient’s **Patient ID**.

The doctor enters the Patient ID into the system.

The system sends a one-time password (OTP) to the patient.

The patient shares the OTP with the doctor.

The doctor enters the OTP in the system.

If the OTP is valid, the doctor gains access to the patient’s records.

---

## QR Code Access

The patient generates a QR code from their profile.

The doctor scans the QR code using the system.

The patient receives a confirmation request.

The patient approves the request.

Once approved, the doctor gains access to the patient’s records.

---

# 8. Trusted Patient Relationship

Once a patient grants access to a doctor, the doctor becomes part of the patient's **trusted doctors list**.

This allows the doctor to access the patient's records in the future without repeating the authentication process.

Doctors can maintain a list of patients they have treated.

This allows doctors to easily revisit patient records during follow-up visits.

---

# 9. Viewing Patient Medical History

Once access is granted, the doctor can view the patient's complete medical history stored in the system.

This history may include:

Hospital visits
Past prescriptions
Diagnosis records
Medical reports

Records are organized in a structured hierarchy based on hospitals and visit dates.

This organization helps doctors quickly understand the patient’s treatment history.

---

# 10. Doctor Role During Consultation

During a consultation, the doctor examines the patient and determines the diagnosis and treatment plan.

In real-world healthcare settings, doctors typically write prescriptions on paper rather than entering them directly into a digital system.

This platform follows a similar workflow.

After writing the prescription, the doctor hands the prescription to the nurse or medical assistant.

The nurse then converts the prescription into a digital medical record.

---

# 11. Nurse Interaction

Doctors work closely with nurses during the prescription creation process.

Nurses are responsible for:

Entering structured medical information
Uploading prescription documents
Creating visit records

Doctors provide the medical instructions, while nurses perform the digital data entry.

This reflects real-world hospital workflows.

---

# 12. Viewing Created Records

Doctors can view all records associated with the patients they have treated.

These records may include:

Diagnosis information
Prescription details
Uploaded prescription documents
Visit dates

Doctors can review these records when patients return for follow-up visits.

---

# 13. Doctor Restrictions

Doctors must not be able to modify or delete existing medical records once they are created.

This restriction ensures the integrity and reliability of medical data.

If corrections are needed, new records can be created instead of altering previous ones.

This maintains a clear historical timeline of treatments.

---

# 14. Doctor Patient List

The doctor dashboard should maintain a list of patients treated by the doctor.

This list allows doctors to quickly locate patient records during future consultations.

Each patient entry may include:

Patient name
Last visit date
Associated hospital

This section functions as a **doctor's personal patient directory**.

---

# 15. Viewing Audit Logs

Doctors should be able to view logs related to actions performed on patient records associated with them.

Examples of logged events include:

Prescription record created
Record edited
Record accessed

These logs increase transparency and accountability within the system.

---

# 16. Doctor Role in the Overall System

Doctors serve as the **primary healthcare providers** within the platform.

They interact directly with patients and medical records while collaborating with nurses to create digital prescriptions.

Doctors use the system to:

Access patient data
Track treatment history
Manage patient relationships

However, they do not control hospital administration and cannot modify historical medical data.

Nurse / Medical Assistant Role – Functional Description
1. Overview of the Nurse Role

The nurse (also referred to as a medical assistant) is responsible for digitizing medical prescriptions and treatment details after a patient consultation.

In real-world healthcare environments, doctors usually write prescriptions on paper after examining a patient. Nurses or medical assistants then handle administrative tasks such as recording treatment information into hospital systems.

The platform follows a similar workflow.

Within the system, nurses act as the primary data entry operators for medical visit records. They convert the doctor’s prescription and instructions into structured digital records that become part of the patient’s medical history.

Nurses therefore play a critical role in ensuring that patient medical records are accurate, structured, and accessible.

2. Nurse Profile Creation

Nurses create their own independent professional accounts on the platform.

These accounts are not created by hospitals.

During registration, the nurse provides basic professional information such as:

Full name
Email address
Password
Professional role (Nurse / Medical Assistant)

The nurse account exists independently of any hospital.

This allows nurses to maintain the same professional identity even if they move to a different hospital.

3. Nurse Login

Nurses log in using their email and password.

After successful login, the nurse is redirected to the Nurse Dashboard.

The nurse dashboard is designed primarily for creating and managing patient visit records.

4. Joining a Hospital

Although nurses have independent profiles, they must associate with a hospital in order to participate in patient treatment workflows.

To join a hospital, the nurse must enter the hospital code generated by the hospital.

The process works as follows:

The nurse logs into the platform.

The nurse enters the hospital code.

The system sends a join request to the hospital.

The hospital administrator reviews the request.

If the hospital approves the request, the nurse becomes associated with that hospital.

Once connected, the nurse can be assigned to doctors within that hospital.

5. Nurse Dashboard Overview

The nurse dashboard provides access to the tools required for managing digital medical records.

Typical sections of the nurse dashboard include:

Nurse profile
Associated hospital
Assigned doctors
Patient visit record creation
Audit logs

The dashboard should focus on efficiently creating patient visit records during hospital consultations.

6. Working with Doctors

Nurses assist doctors during the consultation workflow.

Hospitals can assign nurses to specific doctors.

This relationship determines which doctor the nurse supports when creating medical records.

For example, a nurse assigned to a dermatologist may assist that doctor in entering dermatology-related patient records.

The nurse can create visit records only for doctors they are assigned to.

7. Creating a Patient Visit Record

The most important responsibility of the nurse is creating a patient visit record.

This record represents the outcome of a doctor–patient consultation.

The process typically begins after the doctor has examined the patient and written the prescription.

The nurse then converts this prescription into a digital format inside the system.

8. Patient Record Creation Workflow

The record creation process typically follows these steps.

The patient visits a doctor.

The patient grants the doctor access to their medical records.

The doctor examines the patient and writes a prescription.

The nurse receives the prescription.

The nurse creates a new visit record in the system.

The nurse enters medical information from the prescription.

The nurse uploads a digital copy of the prescription.

The system stores the visit record in the patient's medical history.

9. Information Entered by the Nurse

When creating a visit record, the nurse enters key information related to the consultation.

This may include:

Patient identification
Hospital identification
Doctor identification
Date of visit
Diagnosis information
Prescription details
Next visit date (if provided)

The nurse may also upload the original prescription as a document.

This document becomes part of the patient’s permanent medical record.

10. Structured Medical Fields

In addition to uploading prescription documents, the nurse may enter structured medical information.

Structured data allows the system to analyze and track medical conditions over time.

Examples may include:

Blood sugar level
Blood pressure
Weight
Heart rate

These values can later be used to generate health graphs and trends for patients and doctors.

11. Condition-Specific Medical Fields

Some medical conditions require additional information.

For example, when a patient is diagnosed with diabetes, specific medical fields may need to be recorded, such as blood sugar readings or HbA1c levels.

The system may provide predefined templates for certain medical conditions.

These templates allow nurses to easily enter relevant medical information without manually defining fields each time.

The detailed design of these condition-specific templates will be defined separately.

12. Uploading Prescription Documents

Nurses should upload a digital version of the doctor’s prescription.

This document may be:

A scanned document
A photograph converted to PDF
A digital prescription file

The uploaded file serves as an official reference for the treatment instructions provided by the doctor.

13. Record Finalization

Once the nurse finishes entering all required information, the visit record is saved.

The record becomes part of the patient’s permanent medical history.

Patients and doctors can later view this record from their dashboards.

14. Nurse Restrictions

Nurses have limited access to patient data.

They can create visit records but should not be able to:

Modify existing historical records created by other users
Delete patient records
Access unrelated patient data

These restrictions ensure that patient medical records remain secure and trustworthy.

15. Audit Logging

All nurse activities should be recorded in the system’s audit logs.

Examples of logged actions include:

Creating a patient visit record
Uploading a prescription document
Updating record details

Each log entry should contain:

Action performed
User performing the action
Timestamp
Additional details

These logs ensure transparency and accountability.

16. Nurse Role in the Overall System

The nurse is responsible for transforming physical prescriptions into digital medical records.

Without this role, the system would not be able to capture structured medical data from hospital visits.

Nurses act as the bridge between the doctor's diagnosis and the digital health record system.

They ensure that medical data is accurately recorded and stored for future reference.

Dynamic Medical Fields Based on Doctor Specialization

When a nurse creates a new patient visit record, the system should automatically determine which medical fields are required based on the doctor’s specialization.

Each doctor profile contains a specialization such as:

Dermatology
Cardiology
Orthopedics
Endocrinology
General Medicine

Different medical specializations require different types of information during patient consultations.

For example, a diabetes consultation requires different medical data than a dermatology consultation.

Because of this, the system should dynamically generate the appropriate data entry fields for the nurse.

How the System Determines Required Fields

When the nurse begins creating a new patient visit record, the system performs the following steps:

The nurse selects the patient.

The nurse selects the doctor responsible for the consultation.

The system retrieves the doctor’s specialization from the doctor profile.

Based on the specialization, the system loads the predefined medical template associated with that specialization.

This template determines which structured fields the nurse must fill.

Example Workflow

For example, if the doctor specialization is Endocrinology (Diabetes), the system may generate fields such as:

Blood sugar level
HbA1c value
Medication prescribed
Diet recommendations
Next follow-up date

If the doctor specialization is Dermatology, the generated fields may include:

Affected skin area
Skin condition type
Medication prescribed
Duration of treatment

These predefined templates ensure that the nurse captures the relevant clinical information required for each type of medical condition.

Nurse Data Entry Process

After the required fields are generated, the nurse fills in the medical information provided by the doctor.

The nurse may also add optional notes if necessary.

Once the structured fields are completed, the nurse must upload the prescription document.

Prescription Document Upload

The system allows nurses to upload a prescription document in one of the following ways:

Uploading a PDF file
Providing a link to a PDF document
Uploading a scanned prescription converted into a PDF

This document represents the original prescription written by the doctor and serves as the official reference for the treatment.

The uploaded prescription becomes part of the patient’s permanent medical record.

Finalizing the Record

After filling the structured medical fields and uploading the prescription document, the nurse submits the visit record.

The system then stores the record in the patient's medical history.

The record is linked to:

Patient
Hospital
Doctor
Nurse
Visit date

Once saved, the record becomes visible to the patient and the doctor.

Purpose of Dynamic Field Generation

This dynamic field generation system ensures that:

Medical data entry remains consistent across hospitals.

Relevant medical information is captured for each specialization.

Health metrics can be tracked and visualized later.

The nurse interface remains simple and organized.
